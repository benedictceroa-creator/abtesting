Prepare for Work

Your First Job Survival Guide

Everything you need to know before and after joining your first job — pay slips, EPF, your rights, and how to spot a scam.

Week One

Your first week at work

The first few days of any job feel overwhelming. This is completely normal. Here is how to navigate them well — and make a strong first impression that stays with your employer.

📅
Day 1 — Arrive prepared
  • Arrive 10 minutes early. On your very first day, tardiness is unforgivable.
  • Bring all your documents in a clean folder — Aadhaar, certificate, photos, bank details.
  • Dress neatly and professionally — even if it is "just training day."
  • Greet everyone with a smile and "Good morning Sir/Madam."
  • Carry a pen and a small notebook to write down instructions.
👂
Day 1 — Listen more than you speak
  • Ask questions, but not too many at once. Write them down and ask when appropriate.
  • Do not volunteer opinions about how things "should" be done on your first day.
  • Observe how people communicate — formal or informal, English or Telugu?
  • Find out who your direct supervisor is and speak to them first for any need.
  • Thank your manager at the end of day one for welcoming you.
📋
First week — Understand the rules
  • Ask where to find the attendance register and how to mark your arrival.
  • Confirm exact working hours, break timings, and which day is your weekly off.
  • Ask how to request leave — through whom, with how much notice, and in which format.
  • Find out if there is a probation period and what is expected of you during it.
  • Ask about the uniform policy (if any) — make sure you comply from day two.
🤝
Building relationships
  • Learn your colleagues' names and use them — it builds trust quickly.
  • Offer to help when you see a colleague struggling — do not wait to be asked.
  • Do not get involved in office gossip or complaints, especially as a new employee.
  • Be respectful to everyone regardless of their position — peons and cleaning staff included.
  • Eat lunch with colleagues when possible — relationships built over food are strong ones.
💪
Performing your work
  • Always do your current task better than required — employers promote people who over-deliver.
  • If you finish a task early, do not sit idle — ask what you can do next.
  • Report problems to your supervisor immediately — never hide a mistake.
  • If you do not know how to do something, say "I am not sure — could you show me once?" Never guess and damage work.
  • Keep your workspace clean and organised at all times.
📱
Phone and personal conduct
  • Keep your phone on silent during working hours. Check messages only during break time.
  • Do not use the employer's wi-fi for personal browsing or social media.
  • Do not take photographs inside the workplace without permission.
  • Personal calls should be taken outside, briefly, and only for urgent matters.
  • Never speak negatively about your employer on social media — even privately.
⚠️ Common mistakes new employees make — and how to avoid them
  • ✕
    Being late after day one. Punctuality is how employers measure reliability. One late arrival without a reason plants doubt that never fully disappears.
  • ✕
    Taking too many sick days in the first 3 months. During probation, attendance is watched closely. If you are genuinely unwell, call in early and provide a medical certificate.
  • ✕
    Saying "that was not my job." Freshers who refuse tasks outside their exact description are seen as difficult. In your first year, be willing to help wherever needed.
  • ✕
    Leaving without notice. Even if the job is not right, give the required notice period. Leaving without notice means you will not get a reference letter — and in small industries, word travels fast.
  • ✕
    Not following up on documents. Collect your offer letter, appointment letter, pay slips, and EPF UAN number within the first month. These are your rights and you will need them.

What do the deductions mean?
✓ Normal ⚠ Ask questions ✕ Never allowed
  • ✓
    PF (Provident Fund): 12% of your Basic Salary. Goes into your retirement savings account. You can withdraw it when you leave the job or retire. NORMAL.
  • ✓
    ESI (Employee State Insurance): 0.75% of Gross Salary. Provides free medical treatment for you and your family, maternity benefits, and sickness pay. NORMAL.
  • ✓
    Professional Tax: A small state tax (₹150–200/month). Every state has it. NORMAL.
  • ⚠
    TDS / Income Tax: Only applies if your annual income exceeds ₹2.5 lakh (about ₹20,833/month). If you earn less, this should be ₹0. If they are deducting it on a lower salary, ask for an explanation.
  • ⚠
    Uniform deduction: Some companies deduct the cost of uniforms from your first salary. Ask in advance so you are prepared. Confirm how much and whether it will be refunded.
  • ⚠
    Advances: If you took an advance salary, it should be deducted in a way that was agreed in writing. Do not accept verbal agreements on advances.
  • ✕
    Training fee deductions: An employer CANNOT deduct fees for mandatory on-the-job training. If this happens, it may be illegal. Report to the Labour Department.
  • ✕
    Damage deductions without proof: An employer cannot deduct money claiming you damaged something without proper investigation and your written consent.

Your Savings & Insurance

EPF and ESI — what they are and why they matter

Many first-time employees see PF and ESI as "deductions" and feel cheated. In reality, they are the two most important financial benefits you will receive as an employee in India. Understanding them will change how you see them.

EPF — Employee Provident Fund
Managed by EPFO (Government of India)
You and your employer each contribute 12% of your basic salary every month into a savings account in your name.
The government adds 8.15% annual interest on your total balance — higher than most bank savings accounts.
You can withdraw your EPF balance when you leave a job, retire, or in an emergency (housing, medical, education).
When you change jobs, you transfer your EPF balance — you do not lose it.
Applies to all companies with 20 or more employees and to employees earning below ₹15,000/month basic.
ESI — Employee State Insurance
Managed by ESIC (Government of India)
You pay 0.75% of gross salary; your employer pays 3.25%. Together this funds your medical insurance.
You and your entire family get FREE medical treatment at ESIC hospitals and dispensaries.
If you fall sick and cannot work, you receive 70% of your salary as sickness benefit for up to 91 days per year.
Maternity benefit: full salary for 26 weeks of maternity leave, paid by ESIC (not your employer).
Applies to companies with 10+ employees where gross salary is ₹21,000/month or less.

How to Access Your EPF

Getting and using your UAN number

1
Ask your employer for your UAN (Universal Account Number)
Your employer must give you your UAN within the first month of joining. The UAN is a 12-digit number linked to your EPF account for life. Keep it safe — it does not change even when you change jobs.
2
Activate your UAN on the EPFO portal
Go to unifiedportal-mem.epfindia.gov.in → click "Activate UAN" → enter your UAN, Aadhaar, mobile number and date of birth. You will receive an OTP to activate. Once active, you can check your balance anytime.
3
Check your balance by SMS
Send an SMS: EPFOHO UAN ENG to 7738299899 from your registered mobile number. You will receive your latest balance by return SMS. No internet needed.
4
When you leave a job — transfer, do not withdraw
When you join a new employer, ask them to link your existing UAN. This transfers your old EPF balance to continue earning interest. Only withdraw your EPF if you are unemployed for 2 months or more — withdrawing loses the employer's contribution and the government interest if done too early.
ESI Hospital in Hyderabad: ESIC Model Hospital, Sanathnagar, Hyderabad — 040-23810801. Show your ESI card (or ESIC app) at any ESI dispensary for free OPD treatment for you and your family.

Know Your Rights

Your legal rights as an employee

As an employee in India you have rights guaranteed by law. Knowing them does not make you a difficult employee — it makes you a protected one.

⏰
Working Hours
Factories Act / Shops & Establishments Act
Max 8–9 hours/day
Max 48 hours/week

Overtime beyond these hours must be paid at double the normal wage rate. You cannot be forced to work overtime without extra pay. A 30-minute break must be given after every 5 hours of continuous work.

🗓️
Leave Entitlements
Telangana Shops & Establishments Act
1 weekly off
12 days sick/casual leave per year

After completing 240 days of work in a year, you earn earned leave (1 day for every 20 days worked). Unused leave can accumulate up to 30 days. Leaves must be given in writing by the employer each year.

🤱
Maternity Leave
Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Act 2017
26 weeks paid leave

Women employees who have worked for at least 80 days in the 12 months before delivery are entitled to 26 weeks of fully paid maternity leave. ESI pays this benefit — your employer cannot refuse it. An employer cannot fire or deny promotion due to pregnancy.

🛡️
Protection from Harassment
PoSH Act 2013

Every workplace with 10+ employees must have an Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) for sexual harassment complaints. You have the right to complain and be protected from retaliation. If your workplace has no ICC, report to the Local Complaints Committee in your district.

Helpline: 181 (Women's Helpline, Telangana)

📝
Notice Period & Termination
Telangana Shops & Establishments Act

After completing 3 months of service, an employer must give you 30 days' notice or 30 days' pay before dismissal (unless for serious misconduct). You must also give the same notice when resigning. You cannot be dismissed for filing a legal complaint or for pregnancy.

💳
Right to a Pay Slip
Payment of Wages Act

Your employer must give you a written or digital pay slip every month. Wages must be paid on or before the 10th of the following month. You cannot be asked to sign for an amount different from what you received. Wages cannot be paid in alcohol or goods instead of money.

⚠️ If your rights are being violated — how to report
  • 1
    First, speak to your employer or HR in writing (even a WhatsApp message is a record). Keep all messages.
  • 2
    If unresolved: contact the Telangana Labour Department — file a complaint at labour.telangana.gov.in or visit the District Labour Office in Secunderabad.
  • 3
    For EPF disputes: call EPFO helpline 1800-118-005 (free, Monday–Friday 9am–6pm).
  • 4
    For wage theft or dismissal without notice: approach the Labour Court — filing is free for workers. A union or legal aid society can help you draft the complaint.

Protect Yourself

How to spot a job scam

Job scams target freshers specifically — because freshers are eager, less experienced, and often don't know what a real employer looks like. These red flags will protect you.

🚨
They ask you to pay any money — for registration, training, uniform deposit, ID card, or "processing fees." Real employers NEVER charge money to hire you. Any payment demand before or on the first day is a scam. Leave immediately.
🚨
The salary offered is shockingly high — ₹30,000–₹50,000/month for a fresher with no experience. No legitimate employer offers this to someone just out of vocational training. If it sounds too good to be true, it is.
🚨
They contacted you without you applying — via unknown WhatsApp number, Instagram DM, or phone call. Genuine employers post jobs publicly or contact through placement centres. Unsolicited "job offers" from strangers are almost always fraudulent.
🚨
The job requires you to travel to another city before you have signed any papers or met the employer in person. Traffickers use fake job offers. Never travel to another city or state for a job without a written offer letter on company letterhead, confirmed independently.
🚨
They ask for your Aadhaar, bank account details, or OTP before you have joined or received an offer letter. Your Aadhaar and bank details can be misused for fraud. Never share OTPs with anyone — not even with someone claiming to be from a company.
🚨
The interview happens only on WhatsApp or Telegram — you never meet anyone in person or via verified video call. Most genuine employers conduct at least one in-person or verified video interview. Text-only "interviews" are a serious red flag.
🚨
They ask you to keep the "job offer" secret from your family. Any genuine employer is comfortable with you discussing the job with your parents or guardian. Secrecy is a manipulation tactic.

✓ What a real employer looks like

✅
They have a physical address you can visit before accepting. A real company has a real place — a factory, a clinic, a shop, an office. Visit and confirm before you commit.
✅
They give you a written offer letter on company letterhead with the salary, role, location, and joining date clearly stated. Verbal offers are not enough.
✅
They are comfortable with you taking time to decide and to show the offer to your family. Pressure to "decide right now" or "join today" is a red flag.
✅
They register you for EPF and ESI if they have 10+ employees — this is a legal requirement. An employer who refuses to register you is operating illegally.
✅
They pay salary on time, every month into your bank account with a pay slip. Cash-only payments with no record are often used to avoid legal obligations.

Government Support

Government schemes you can apply for

These schemes are free to apply for and are specifically designed for people from vocational training backgrounds like yours.

🏭
PM Mudra Yojana (PMMY)
Loans up to ₹10 lakh for small businesses — no collateral needed for loans up to ₹10,000 (Shishu category). For tailoring boutiques, beauty parlours, electrical repair shops, or any small service business. Apply at any government bank or NBFC. No processing fee. Central Government
👩‍💼
Telangana WE-HUB (Women Entrepreneurs)
Telangana state-run incubator for women entrepreneurs. Provides mentorship, funding support, market access, and free workspace in Hyderabad. If you have completed training and want to start your own beauty parlour, tailoring unit, or training centre, WE-HUB can support you. Telangana StateWomen Only
🎓
PMKVY — PM Kaushal Vikas Yojana
If you want to upgrade your skills further, PMKVY offers free short-term training courses (usually 3–6 months) in trades similar to what you studied. On completion you receive a government certificate and placement assistance. Check NSDC (nationalskillindia.gov.in) for courses near Secunderabad. Central Government
🏦
Standup India
Bank loans between ₹10 lakh and ₹1 crore for SC/ST and women entrepreneurs to set up greenfield enterprises in manufacturing, services, or trade. One loan per bank branch is reserved for each category. If you are planning a larger business venture, speak to your nearest SBI, Canara Bank, or nationalised bank branch. Central GovernmentWomen / SC / ST
🏥
Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (Ayushman Bharat)
Free hospitalisation coverage of up to ₹5 lakh per family per year at empanelled government and private hospitals. If your family is below or near the poverty line, check your eligibility at pmjay.gov.in or call 14555. ESIC covers you while employed — Ayushman Bharat covers family members not covered by ESI. Central Government
