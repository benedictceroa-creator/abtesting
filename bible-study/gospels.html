Shared and unique

Three that look alike, and one that stands apart

Matthew, Mark and Luke are called the Synoptic Gospels, from a Greek word meaning “seen together”. They tell much of the same story, often in very similar words, in a similar order, and mostly in Galilee.

John wrote later and chose differently. Almost everything in his Gospel is found nowhere else: long conversations, the seven signs, and Jesus' visits to Jerusalem for the feasts.

Which was written first?

The early church generally listed Matthew first. Many evangelical scholars today think Mark was written first and that Matthew and Luke drew on it as well as on their own sources. Either way, Luke tells us he carefully used eyewitness accounts (Luke 1:1-4), and Bible-believing scholars agree that all four are trustworthy.
