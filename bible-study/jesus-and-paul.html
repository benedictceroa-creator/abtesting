Jesus & the Early Church: Bible Timeline & Atlas

From the birth of Jesus in Bethlehem, through his ministry in Galilee, his death and resurrection in Jerusalem, and the coming of the Spirit, to the church spreading through Judea, Samaria and the Roman world on Paul's journeys, until the gospel reaches Rome. Sources and method

Every entry on this page lists the Scripture passages it is drawn from, and where Bible-believing scholars disagree, we say so.

  • The birth of Jesus. Matthew places it before the death of Herod the Great (Matthew 2:1, 19), which most scholars date to 4 BC, so Jesus was born about 6–4 BC. (Our calendar's AD 1 was miscalculated in the 6th century.)
  • The ministry and the cross. Luke dates John's preaching to the fifteenth year of Tiberius (Luke 3:1), that is AD 28–29, and John's Gospel follows Jesus' ministry through several Passovers (John 2:13; 6:4; 11:55). This page dates the crucifixion to Friday 3 April AD 33. Many evangelical scholars prefer 7 April AD 30; on that view Jesus' ministry and the first years of the church move about three years earlier.
  • Paul. The order comes from Acts and from Galatians 1–2. Two Roman dates fix it: Gallio was proconsul of Achaia in AD 51–52 (the Delphi inscription), and Festus replaced Felix in about AD 59. Paul's later travels and the dates of his and Peter's deaths rest on his later letters and early Christian writers, and are marked disputed.
  • When the books were written follows the traditional view held by most evangelical scholars, as in the Bible Book Explorer. John's Gospel, letters and Revelation were written later, about AD 85–95.
  • Evidence outside the Bible is noted where it bears on the text: the Roman historians Tacitus and Suetonius, the Jewish historian Josephus, and inscriptions naming Pilate, Gallio and others.
  • Places and their confidence levels come from the OpenBible.info Bible Geocoding Data (CC BY 4.0).
  • Maps. The Roman provinces and client kingdoms are simplified outlines. Most routes follow the roads and sea lanes found in standard Bible atlases; the Gospels and Acts name the places but rarely the roads.
